# Churn Intervention

**A repeatable save play for accounts that are slipping**

When an account goes at-risk, the instinct is to react fast. The better move is to diagnose the real reason first, then act before the renewal window closes.

This playbook covers when to run the play, how to find the actual cause, and how to know when a save is no longer worth your time.

---

## 1. When to run this

Run the play the moment an account goes Red in renewal risk governance, or when one of these shows up:

- Usage drops sharply (seats, value activity, or logins)
- The champion leaves or goes quiet
- A key integration is removed
- Support friction stacks up (escalations, slow resolutions, a painful bug)
- A budget or economic signal lands (hiring freeze, reorg, "we're reviewing our tools")

---

## 2. Diagnose the real reason

The stated reason is rarely the real one. "Too expensive" usually means "we never proved value."

- Map the symptom to a cause: low adoption, lost champion, unmet original goal, competitor, product gap, or budget
- Pull the health-scoring trends to separate a real drop from noise
- Write the cause in one sentence. If you can't, you don't understand it yet.

---

## 3. Confirm with the customer

Don't diagnose a save from your dashboard alone.

- Have the direct conversation
- Ask what changed, and what would have to be true for them to stay
- Listen for the economic buyer's view, not only the daily user's

---

## 4. Build the save plan

- Tie it to the original problem you captured during onboarding. That problem is your strongest argument.
- Pick one quick win you can land in two weeks to rebuild momentum
- Set a clear success measure and a date

---

## 5. Multi-thread

A one-champion account is a single point of failure.

- Build a second and third relationship inside the account
- Reach the economic buyer before the renewal window, not inside it
- Map who benefits if you stay and who signs the renewal. They are not always the same person.

---

## 6. Re-establish value

- Re-onboard the feature tied to their core problem
- Run an executive business review: remind leadership what they bought and what it has returned
- Put the proof in their language: their KPI, their dollars, their risk reduced

---

## 7. Escalate internally

- Bring in your exec sponsor for an exec-to-exec conversation
- Ask product for a specific, dated commitment if a gap is the blocker
- Loop in pricing or packaging help early if cost is the real issue

---

## 8. Know when it's lost

Not every account is savable, and over-investing in a lost one costs you two healthy ones.

- When the problem is gone (the team, the budget, the use case), run a clean offboarding
- Leave the door open. A respectful exit is the start of the win-back.

---

## 9. Run it against the clock

| Time to renewal | Focus |
|-----------------|-------|
| 90+ days | Diagnose, confirm, start the quick win |
| 30-60 days | Prove value, multi-thread, escalate if still Red |
| Inside 30 days | Secure the economic buyer and the paperwork; stop opening new workstreams |

---

## 10. Connection to other playbooks

- **Renewal risk governance:** Every Red triggers this play.
- **Health scoring:** The trend data behind the diagnosis.
- **Alerts:** The early-warning signals that fire the play.
- **Onboarding:** The original problem you defend.

---

## Final thought

You can't save an account you diagnosed wrong. Find the real reason, prove value against it, and know when to walk.
