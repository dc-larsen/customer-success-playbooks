# Renewal Risk Governance Playbook

## 🎯 Purpose
Make renewal risk visible early enough to act on it. This playbook replaces ad-hoc renewal notes with a structured, repeatable process leadership can read and act on, so no renewal is a surprise.

## 🔥 The Problem It Solves
- Renewal risk usually lives in the CSM's head and surfaces two weeks out, when it's too late to fix
- "How's that renewal looking?" gets a different answer every time you ask
- Leadership can't help with risks they can't see

Structured risk data fixes all three. If every at-risk account is recorded the same way, anyone can scan the book and know where to spend time.

## 🧱 The Risk Record
Track these fields on every account, the same way every time:

- **Renewal date** and **ARR** (the stakes and the clock)
- **Risk level**: Green, Yellow, or Red (defined below)
- **Risk category**, the *why*, from a fixed list: champion change, low adoption, budget or economic, competitor, product gap, support friction, M&A or reorg
- **Forecast**: Commit, Likely, or At-Risk
- **Risk narrative**: three sentences covering what's happening, why, and what you're doing about it
- **Next step**, with an **owner** and a **date**

The fixed category list matters. Free-text reasons can't be rolled up. A fixed list lets leadership see "four of our Reds are champion changes" and respond at the pattern level.

## 🚦 Risk Levels
Define the tiers so "Yellow" means the same thing for every CSM:

- **Green**: adoption healthy, champion engaged, no open blockers. The default for accounts tracking to renew.
- **Yellow**: one real risk signal (a usage dip, a quiet champion, a competitor mentioned). Needs a plan, not yet an escalation.
- **Red**: the renewal is genuinely in question. Triggers the [Churn Intervention](../churn-intervention/churn-intervention.md) play and leadership involvement.

A level with no required action is just a label. Each tier carries an expectation.

## ⏰ Cadence
- **Quarterly risk review** across the whole book with leadership. Walk every Yellow and Red, confirm the narrative and the next step.
- **Monthly on Reds.** They move fast, and a quarter is too long to wait.
- **Update on change.** Change the record the day the signal appears. The review confirms risk, it doesn't discover it.

## 🆘 Escalation Thresholds
Pull in leadership or the exec sponsor when:
- An account goes **Red**, or a Yellow sits above your ARR escalation bar
- The risk is **outside your control**: pricing, a product gap, an executive relationship
- You need a **cross-functional commitment**: a product date, a discount, an exec-to-exec call

Escalate with the narrative and a specific ask, not just "this one's at risk."

## 📈 Tie Risk to the Forecast
- Roll risk levels into the renewal forecast. A Red rarely forecasts as Commit.
- Track forecast accuracy over time. If Reds keep renewing, your bar is too sensitive. If Commits keep slipping, it isn't sensitive enough.
- The point is a forecast leadership can trust, not an optimistic one.

## 🔗 Integration with Other Playbooks
- **[Health Scoring](../health-scoring/health-scoring.md)** → usage and integration trends feed the risk level
- **[Proactive Alerts](../alerts/alerts.md)** → a "risk field updated" alert puts the change in front of the team immediately
- **[Churn Intervention](../churn-intervention/churn-intervention.md)** → every Red runs the save play
- **[Onboarding](../onboarding/onboarding.md)** → the problem you captured at onboarding is the bar you defend at renewal
