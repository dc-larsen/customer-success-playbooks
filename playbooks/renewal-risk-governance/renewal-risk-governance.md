# Renewal Risk Governance

**How to make renewal risk visible before it's too late to act**

Most renewal risk lives in the CSM's head and surfaces two weeks before the renewal, when the options are gone. Leadership finds out last, and only once you're already losing.

This playbook covers how to record risk the same way every time, review it on a cadence, and escalate it early enough to change the outcome.

---

## 1. Why structured risk beats notes

Free-text renewal notes can't be compared, rolled up, or acted on by anyone but the person who wrote them. "How's that renewal looking?" gets a different answer every time you ask.

Structured risk fixes that. When every at-risk account is recorded the same way, anyone can scan the book, see where the revenue is exposed, and help.

---

## 2. The risk record

Track these fields on every account, the same way every time:

| Field | What it captures |
|-------|------------------|
| Renewal date, ARR | The clock and the stakes |
| Risk level | Green, Yellow, Red |
| Risk category | The *why*, from a fixed list |
| Forecast | Commit, Likely, At-Risk |
| Risk narrative | Three sentences: what's happening, why, what you're doing |
| Next step | The action, with an owner and a date |

Use a fixed category list, not free text: champion change, low adoption, budget or economic, competitor, product gap, support friction, M&A or reorg. Fixed categories roll up. Leadership can see "four of our Reds are champion changes" and respond at the pattern level.

---

## 3. Risk levels

Define the tiers so "Yellow" means the same thing for every CSM.

| Level | Meaning | Required action |
|-------|---------|-----------------|
| Green | Adoption healthy, champion engaged, no blockers | Normal cadence |
| Yellow | One real risk signal (usage dip, quiet champion, competitor named) | A written plan and a next step |
| Red | Renewal is genuinely in question | Churn intervention play, leadership involved |

A level with no required action is just a label. Each tier carries an expectation.

---

## 4. Cadence

- **Quarterly:** review the whole book with leadership. Walk every Yellow and Red, confirm the narrative and the next step.
- **Monthly:** review every Red. They move fast, and a quarter is too long to wait.
- **On change:** update the record the day the signal appears. The review confirms risk, it doesn't discover it.

---

## 5. Escalation thresholds

Pull in leadership or the exec sponsor when:

- An account goes Red, or a Yellow sits above your ARR escalation bar
- The risk is outside your control: pricing, a product gap, an executive relationship
- You need a cross-functional commitment: a product date, a discount, an exec-to-exec call

Escalate with the narrative and a specific ask, not "this one's at risk."

---

## 6. Tie risk to the forecast

Roll risk levels into the renewal forecast. A Red rarely forecasts as Commit.

Track forecast accuracy over time:
- If Reds keep renewing, your bar is too sensitive
- If Commits keep slipping, it isn't sensitive enough

The goal is a forecast leadership can trust, not an optimistic one.

---

## 7. Connection to other playbooks

- **Health scoring:** Usage and integration trends feed the risk level and the category.
- **Alerts:** A "risk field updated" alert puts the change in front of the team immediately.
- **Churn intervention:** Every Red runs the save play.
- **Onboarding:** The problem you captured at onboarding is the bar you defend at renewal.

---

## Final thought

A renewal you can forecast is one you started managing a quarter early. Write the risk down the day you see it, in a format someone else can act on.
