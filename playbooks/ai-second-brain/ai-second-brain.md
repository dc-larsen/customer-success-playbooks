# AI Second Brain with Obsidian

**A markdown knowledge base your AI can read and grow**

Account knowledge lives in your head, in scattered notes, and in old Slack threads, so it never compounds. A second brain is a folder of markdown your AI assistant reads and writes with you, so what you learn once stays found.

This playbook covers how to build one and keep it useful.

---

## 1. Why markdown, why Obsidian

Plain markdown is portable, future-proof, and something an AI assistant can read and edit directly. Obsidian sits on top of a folder of `.md` files and turns the links between them into a graph you can browse. Any plain-text editor works; the format is the point.

---

## 2. Structure it in three layers

- **Sources:** raw material you drop in (call notes, tickets, doc snippets). Never rewrite these.
- **Concept pages:** synthesized notes on one topic, customer, or product area
- **An index:** a table of contents linking the concept pages

Link pages to each other liberally. The links are what make it more than a folder.

---

## 3. The loop that makes it compound

- Capture a source after a call or a ticket
- Have the assistant synthesize it into the right concept page
- When you answer a question, cite the page you used
- File the good answer back as a new or updated page

Each pass makes the next question faster to answer.

---

## 4. What to keep in it

Customer and account knowledge, troubleshooting steps, product facts, and your own playbooks. If you looked something up twice, it belongs here.

---

## 5. Keep customer data private

A second brain fills up with customer information. Keep it local or in private storage, and don't sync it anywhere public.

---

## 6. Connection to other playbooks

- **Claude Code Skills:** Build a skill that ingests sources and updates pages.
- **Call Review Automation:** File recurring call feedback so the lessons stick.
- **CLAUDE.md:** Point the assistant at the knowledge base so it reads from it by default.

---

## Final thought

Knowledge you don't write down, you look up again. Capture it once in plain markdown, link it, and let your assistant turn scattered notes into something that compounds.
