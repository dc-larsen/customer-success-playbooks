# Onboarding Metrics

**How to define, track, and improve onboarding success across your customer base**

One great onboarding is luck. A measurable, repeatable one is a process. If you can't say which of your onboardings worked and which stalled, you can't fix the ones that don't.

This playbook covers how to define onboarding success, the metrics that prove it, and how to use them to improve the process itself.

---

## 1. Start from the outcome, not the checklist

"Onboarded" is not "we finished the kickoff deck." It means the customer reached the value they bought you for.

Define one completion bar and use it for every customer:
- The **value milestone** tied to why they bought (the value activity defined in the Onboarding playbook)
- **Deployed and in use** by the intended team, not just one admin
- The original problem is **measurably moving**

If the definition changes per account, you can't compare onboardings or trust the numbers.

---

## 2. The core metrics

| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| Time to First Value (TTFV) | Days from kickoff to the first real value activity |
| Time to Deployment | Days to go-live with the intended team |
| Onboarding cycle time | Kickoff to meeting the completion bar |
| Completion rate | % of customers that hit the bar at all |
| Activation rate | % that reached the value milestone |
| Stalled rate | % stuck past your target window |
| Onboarding CSAT | How the customer felt about the experience |

Watch TTFV first. It predicts retention earlier and better than almost anything else you can measure this soon.

---

## 3. Leading signals vs lagging results

- **Leading** (predict): milestone progress, integrations connected, first value activity in week one. Watch these to step in while you still can.
- **Lagging** (confirm): TTFV, completion rate, and downstream renewal and expansion. Track these to judge whether the process works.

Act on the leading signals; report on the lagging ones.

---

## 4. Set a target per segment

A metric with no target can't flag a problem. Set a target TTFV and cycle time for each segment, because enterprise and SMB won't match.

- Flag any onboarding that crosses its target window
- Treat a crossed target as a trigger to step in, not a number to explain later

---

## 5. Track cohorts, not just accounts

Group onboardings by start month and segment, then compare.

- Is TTFV trending down quarter over quarter, or creeping up?
- Which segment onboards slowest?
- Which **step** do onboardings stall on most often?

One stalled account is a customer issue. The same step stalling across a cohort is a process issue.

---

## 6. Close the loop on the process

The metrics exist to fix onboarding, not to decorate a dashboard.

- When a step keeps stalling, redesign it and give it an owner
- When TTFV creeps up, find what changed: a new integration step, a staffing gap, a product change
- Re-measure after the fix to confirm it worked

---

## 7. Where to track it

Use onboarding projects in your CS platform (Vitally, Gainsight) with dated milestones, or a lightweight tracker if those don't exist:

| Customer | Segment | Kickoff | First value | TTFV (days) | Completed | Stalled step |
|----------|---------|---------|-------------|-------------|-----------|--------------|
| Acme Corp | Enterprise | 2024-01-05 | 2024-01-22 | 17 | Yes | none |
| Beta Inc | SMB | 2024-01-09 | pending | pending | No | SSO setup |

Update it as milestones land, not at quarter close.

---

## 8. Connection to other playbooks

- **Onboarding:** Defines the value activity and completion bar these metrics measure.
- **Health scoring:** Your earliest health signals are the onboarding metrics. A slow start is an early risk.
- **Renewal risk governance:** Slow or incomplete onboarding is an early risk category. Flag it before it becomes a renewal problem.
- **Case studies:** The baseline you capture at onboarding is the "before" in every success story.

---

## Final thought

Onboarding is the highest-impact moment in the customer lifecycle, and the easiest to fake "done" on. Measure it the same way every time, set a target, and fix the step that keeps stalling.
