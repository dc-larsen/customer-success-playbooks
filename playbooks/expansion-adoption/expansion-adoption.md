# Expansion & Adoption

**How to spot expansion early and earn it through deeper adoption**

Expansion is not a quarterly upsell push. It is what happens when a customer uses more of the platform to solve more of their problems. Your job is to drive the adoption that creates real expansion signals, then act on them before the renewal forces the conversation.

This playbook covers where expansion comes from, the signals that predict it, and how to drive the adoption that makes it happen.

---

## 1. Expansion follows value, not pressure

You earn expansion by solving the next problem, not by asking for budget. The strongest expansion is a customer realizing they need more because you helped them win with what they already have.

If an account isn't getting value from what they bought, expansion talk is noise. Fix adoption first.

---

## 2. Where expansion comes from

| Type | Looks like |
|------|------------|
| Seat growth | More users, or a new team on the same product |
| New use case | An adjacent problem a different feature or product solves |
| Tier upgrade | They've outgrown the limits of their current plan |
| New org | Land-and-expand into another department or business unit |

Name which type fits the account. Each one has a different buyer and a different proof.

---

## 3. Signals to watch

- License usage approaching or at the limit (90-100%)
- Strong adoption plus a new team asking for access
- A new problem raised on a call that another part of the platform solves
- Hiring in an area your product serves (read their job posts)
- High health score and a champion with budget influence

These come straight from your alerts and health data. Expansion signals are health signals pointed forward.

---

## 4. Drive the adoption that creates expansion

- Map the platform to their roadmap: which unused capabilities solve goals they've already told you about
- Run targeted enablement on the next high-value feature, not a feature dump
- Tie every new bit of adoption to a measurable win, so it justifies the spend later
- Widen who uses it; more teams means more stickiness and more seats

---

## 5. Multi-thread for the expansion buyer

Expansion usually needs a budget the daily user doesn't control. Build relationships beyond your original champion, and find the person who owns the adjacent problem you're about to solve.

---

## 6. Time it, then hand it off with context

- Surface the opportunity early and loop in the account executive; expansion is a team motion
- Don't wait for the renewal to expand. Mid-term expansion is often a cleaner conversation than a renewal-plus-upsell.
- Bring the data: usage at the limit, the win you delivered, the new use case in their words

---

## 7. Connection to other playbooks

- **Alerts:** License-usage and new-team alerts are your expansion triggers.
- **Health scoring:** Only expand healthy accounts. Pushing more product onto a struggling one accelerates churn.
- **Onboarding:** The original problem and value milestone point you to the next adjacent problem.
- **Case studies:** A proven win is the strongest expansion argument you have.

---

## Final thought

Expansion is adoption that paid off. Drive the next win, watch the usage, and bring the opportunity to sales with the data already in hand.
